Reference 01
You vs Carion / DETR
Carion's thesis is the most mathematically mature of the three references. It formally derives detection as set prediction using bipartite matching with a Hungarian algorithm loss, provides convergence analysis, and validates on COCO against Faster-RCNN. The gap with your work is primarily in formalism and external benchmarking, not in intellectual ambition.
Where Carion beats you
Formal loss derivation. The Hungarian matching loss is mathematically proven to force unique, permutation-invariant predictions. Your SAA metric is operationally defined but lacks probabilistic derivation — why does σ(agent scores) measure epistemic uncertainty? There is no Bayesian justification stated in the thesis.
Standardised benchmark. COCO enables direct SOTA comparison. Carion can say exactly how DETR compares to Faster-RCNN, RetinaNet, and every subsequent work. Your mAP50 = 0.989 is measured on your own pseudo-labels — it is not externally verifiable without access to Colibri and the pipeline.
Ablation isolation. Carion isolates encoder depth, decoder depth, and loss components independently with all else held fixed. Your ablation removes agents but the baseline "monolithic" pipeline still contains six sub-scores — the variable being ablated is not cleanly isolated.
Convergence behaviour. Carion reports training curves and convergence properties. Your iterative self-training convergence (student-teacher alignment 0.404→0.424) is reported but not theoretically analysed — is it guaranteed to converge?
Where you beat Carion
Semantic scope. DETR detects bounding boxes. Your system additionally performs OCR layout parsing, VQA binary screening, scene graph generation, semantic retrieval via FAISS, demographic profiling, and temporal trend analysis. The intellectual scope is 6–7× broader.
Epistemic framing. Carion treats model output as ground truth — a bounding box prediction is either correct or not vs COCO annotations. You treat ALL model outputs as evidence under uncertainty, routing disagreements to human experts. This is a fundamentally more mature philosophy for real-world AI deployment.
Zero-annotation domain adaptation. Carion uses COCO labels. You build a functional 37-class detector for a domain with ZERO existing annotations — starting from mAP50 = 0.005 and reaching 0.989. This is a harder problem and a more practically important result.
HITL as architecture. No CV paper of this era integrates human-in-the-loop routing as a first-class architectural component with precision and efficiency analysis. You show 2.5× reviewer effort reduction — that is a practical contribution Carion does not attempt.

Reference 02
You vs DOtA (CVPR 2025)
DOtA is your most important reference because the structural problem is almost identical: both systems detect objects without manual labels using multi-agent collaboration. DOtA uses physically separate LiDAR vehicles; you use semantically separate AI models. The difference in domain makes direct comparison possible — and you win on semantic richness, lose on methodological tightness.
Where DOtA beats you
Label refinement pipeline. DOtA's three-stage process (preliminary labels → multi-scale encoding → label-internal contrastive learning) is an algorithmic contribution. Your consensus filter (IoU > 0.4, confidence > 0.35, ≥2 model agreement) is sound engineering, but not novel enough to stand alone as an algorithmic contribution.
True agent independence. DOtA's LiDAR vehicles are physically separate — they literally cannot share the same view of the scene. Your agents (DINO, Florence-2, OWL-ViT, CLIP, LLaVA) all process the SAME restored image. Error modes are correlated: if the CycleGAN produces a checkerboard artifact, ALL agents see the same artifact. DOtA formally accounts for this via ICE (Individual Contribution Estimation).
Contrastive learning signal. DOtA uses Label-Internal Contrastive Learning (LICL) to explicitly push high-quality and low-quality pseudo-label representations apart. This produces measurable improvements (+5.18 AP@0.3, +6.60 AP@0.5). Your consensus filtering has no equivalent learned refinement — it discards disagreements rather than learning from them.
Single clean learning objective. DOtA can state: "we train a 3D detector from collaborative LiDAR without labels." You are simultaneously claiming restoration, pseudo-labeling, student training, VQA validation, OCR indexing, and semantic retrieval. A CVPR reviewer will ask: "what is the single algorithmic contribution?"
Where you beat DOtA
Semantic intelligence. DOtA detects 3D bounding boxes around vehicles for autonomous driving. You detect 37 historically meaningful classes, generate captions, answer natural language questions, parse OCR layouts, and enable semantic retrieval. The cognitive complexity is incomparably higher.
Uncertainty as epistemic signal. DOtA uses multi-view consistency to improve label quality — a technical signal. You use inter-agent disagreement to model epistemic uncertainty — a philosophical advance. DOtA's discriminator D classifies label quality; your coordinator routes uncertainty to human experts. Your framing is more general and more honest about AI limitations.
Domain gap solved at the source. DOtA operates on LiDAR scans from modern autonomous vehicles — no domain gap. You solve a 130-year domain gap (1880s photography → modern detector distribution) using generative restoration. This is arguably a harder and more significant contribution.
Knowledge system output. DOtA produces detections. You produce a structured, searchable semantic archive — captions, scene graphs, retrieval indices, OCR layers — that scholars can actually USE for historical research. The downstream value is orders of magnitude higher.

"The most actionable lesson from DOtA: upgrade your consensus filter to include a contrastive learning step. Instead of discarding low-agreement detections, use them as negative examples in a contrastive objective during YOLOv11 fine-tuning. This turns a filtering heuristic into an algorithmic contribution."

Reference 03
You vs Coelho et al. (Springer 2023)
The Coelho et al. paper is the weakest of the three references — it is a system design paper describing a Docker-based multi-agent architecture for radar, image, and acoustic sensor fusion with MQTT messaging. It does not perform ablations, calibration, or formal evaluation. Your thesis is substantially more rigorous and complete on every dimension. The main risk here is framing — make sure your thesis clearly articulates why your architecture is better, not just larger.
Where Coelho is ahead (very little)
Deployment infrastructure. Docker containers + MQTT messaging creates a genuinely deployable production system. Your pipeline is a research prototype running as sequential Python scripts. A production-grade message bus between agents would strengthen the "system" claim in your title.
Sensor modality diversity. Coelho uses radar, image, AND acoustic — physical sensor fusion gives genuinely independent observations. Your modalities (DINO, Florence-2, CLIP, LLaVA) are all visual — they share the same perceptual input and cannot provide the independence that physical sensor separation achieves.
Where you dominate Coelho
Scale and depth. 12,110 images, 37 classes, 6 agent modalities, 5 output layers (detection, caption, VQA, OCR, scene). Coelho describes a design — they do not report dataset scale or detection performance numbers.
Uncertainty quantification. Coelho has no uncertainty or calibration analysis. You have inter-agent disagreement, ECE analysis per agent (VLM ECE = 0.000), HITL routing with precision curves, and break-even analysis. This alone is a complete paper Coelho could not write.
Knowledge system vs detection system. Coelho's goal is detection. Your goal is archival intelligence — the detections are an input to a richer knowledge layer. The semantic retrieval result (P@10 = 1.000 vs 0.090 for keyword) has no equivalent in Coelho.
Domain novelty. Automated enrichment of historical archives is a completely different (and more socially significant) application than autonomous driving sensor fusion. No prior MAS paper has done this for cultural heritage collections.
Honest assessment
What is genuinely original in your work
The reviewer feedback identifies several distinct intellectual contributions that none of the three reference works make. These are your real thesis, and they should be front-loaded in the abstract and introduction.
Genuine contribution
Restoration-First Domain Normalisation
Most prior work adapts the model to the degraded domain (domain-adaptive training, data augmentation). You invert this: normalise the data into the model's native distribution via CycleGAN + Real-ESRGAN + SD refinement. This conceptual inversion is original and practically important — it means ANY modern detector works without retraining.
Genuine contribution
Disagreement as Epistemic Signal
Treating inter-agent disagreement as a proxy for irreducible semantic ambiguity — not just model error — is original. Your finding that 89% of high-disagreement images represent genuine semantic conflict (not implementation bugs) changes how archival uncertainty should be interpreted. Pearson r = −0.799 is strong empirical support.
Genuine contribution
Open-Vocabulary Taxonomy via Captioning + NLP
Deriving 37 domain-specific detection classes from 12,110 unconstrained captions using spaCy frequency analysis — with no manual class definition — is a practical contribution. The Zipf α = 11.77 result shows the vocabulary structure follows archival language distributions. This is not in DETR, DOtA, or Coelho.
Genuine contribution
HITL as First-Class Architecture
Integrating uncertainty-driven routing into the system architecture — not as post-processing — with a rigorous break-even analysis (2.5× effort reduction, P@10 = 1.000) is a practical systems contribution. The review literature does not provide this for archival contexts.
Genuine contribution
VLM Calibration Discovery
The finding that LLaVA-OneVision achieves ECE = 0.000 (perfect calibration) while fusion achieves ECE = 0.319 is a surprising empirical result with practical implications: VLM scores can be used directly as probability estimates without Platt scaling. This is an original observation not reported elsewhere.
Genuine contribution
Archival Semantic Knowledge System
The combination of detection + captioning + VQA + OCR + scene graphs + FAISS retrieval into a structured searchable archive is a systems contribution to digital humanities. Semantic retrieval P@10 = 1.000 vs keyword P@10 = 0.090 is a strong practical result that motivates the whole framework.
Critical gaps
What must be fixed for publication
Critical weakness
Gold Labels: 0/500 filled
Your 500-image human gold worksheet has zero labels. Every HITL efficiency claim, every calibration result, every P@10 result is measured against proxy labels derived from the models themselves. A reviewer will reject this. This must be completed before submission.
Critical weakness
No Formal Uncertainty Theory
σ(agent scores) is used as an uncertainty metric but never theoretically justified. Why is standard deviation of weighted scores a valid uncertainty measure? No Bayesian derivation, no decomposition into aleatoric vs epistemic uncertainty, no conformal prediction interval. Carion's work has this — yours doesn't.
Critical weakness
Agent Independence Not Addressed
All your agents process the same restored image — correlated errors from CycleGAN artifacts propagate to all agents simultaneously. DOtA uses physically separate sensors to guarantee independence. You need to either measure the inter-agent mutual information (to quantify correlation) or acknowledge it as a formal limitation with a mitigation plan.
Critical weakness
No External Dataset Comparison
Your mAP50 = 0.989 is measured on your own pseudo-labels — self-referential validation. You need at least one external historical dataset (ArtDL, People-Art, OmniArt, or WikiArt) to show that the pipeline generalises beyond Colibri. Even a 50-image subset comparison would strengthen the paper substantially.
Structural weakness
Too Many Claims, No Central Thesis
You claim detection, scene understanding, epistemic validation, uncertainty estimation, semantic indexing, historical reasoning, retrieval intelligence, AND archival realism inference. A reviewer cannot evaluate all of these. One central claim must dominate: "cross-agent disagreement is a stronger predictor of archival uncertainty than standalone detector confidence."
Methodological weakness
Consensus Filter is Heuristic, not Algorithmic
IoU > 0.4 + confidence > 0.35 + ≥2 models is solid engineering. But compared to DOtA's Label-Internal Contrastive Learning, it is not a novel algorithmic contribution. The filter discards information rather than learning from it. Upgrading to a learned refinement step (even simple) would close this gap.
