\section{Tier 1 --- Raw Agent Signals}

\notebox{WarnInk}{\textbf{Provenance correction (September 2026).} $A^{\text{object}}$ is \emph{not} the spatial detector's box count, as earlier versions of this reference and the thesis stated. The distilled student's count is zero on all $12{,}110$ images, so a fallback supplied the \textbf{VQA object count} instead: $\min(\text{VQA count}/3,\,1)$ reproduces the deployed column exactly on every image. $A^{\text{vlm}}$ is the VQA object-presence score, so the two share one source. The deployed Tier 1 vector therefore holds \textbf{three} separately-measured signals, not four. No reported number changes --- all were computed from the table as it stands --- but every ensemble claim should be read with this in mind.}

\begin{formulacard}{1.1 \quad Raw Tier 1 agent scores}
Not a derived formula --- the four base measurements everything else is built from. Each is a real model's confidence output on the \emph{restored} image $\mathbf{I}_{\text{refined}}$.
\begin{equation}
A_i^{\text{object}},\ A_i^{\text{scene}},\ A_i^{\text{vlm}},\ A_i^{\text{ocr}} \ \in\ [0,1]
\end{equation}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llp{7.3cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Variable} & \textbf{Model} & \textbf{Measures} \\
\midrule
$A_i^{\text{object}}$ & YOLOv11-seg + SAM & Spatial object-grounding confidence \\
$A_i^{\text{scene}}$ & SigLIP & Zero-shot scene confidence --- read from the \emph{wrong channel}, see 2.10 \\
$A_i^{\text{vlm}}$ & LLaVA-OneVision 7B & VQA description confidence \\
$A_i^{\text{ocr}}$ & Kosmos-2.5 & Grounded inscription/OCR extraction confidence \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\derivedfrom{Thesis \S2.4 ``Canonical Formulation'', Level 1}
\computedby{\texttt{run\_agent\_comparison.py}}
\par\noindent{\small\textbf{Stored in} \texttt{agent\_comparison\_scores.csv} as \texttt{existing\_pipeline\_agent, scene\_agent, vlm\_agent, document\_agent}}
\end{formulacard}

\begin{formulacard}{1.2 \quad The Agreement signal (derived, not a 5th model)}
\begin{equation}
A_i^{\text{agree}} = 1 - \left| A_i^{\text{object}} - A_i^{\text{vlm}} \right|
\end{equation}
Pairwise concordance between the spatial agent and the VQA agent specifically --- not a fifth independent model. High when Object and VLM confidences are close together (whether both high or both low), low when they diverge sharply. This is the signal that lets $U_{\text{SAA}}$ use a 4-term standard deviation over what is architecturally a 3-model ensemble (Object, Scene, VLM): it substitutes for the model OCR would occupy, without OCR ever entering the uncertainty computation.

\derivedfrom{Thesis \S2.4, Level 1 remark}
\computedby{\texttt{run\_agent\_comparison.py} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{agreement\_agent} column}
\par\noindent{\small\textbf{Why it exists:} every empirical script in the project actually uses this 4-signal set, not the architectural 4 (incl. OCR) --- the original write-up missed this and was corrected during this audit.}
\end{formulacard}

\paragraph{The four VQA questions.} Every image was put to LLaVA-OneVision with the same four
prompts, and different downstream signals are derived from different answers:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
  \item \emph{``Identify objects present: person, child, horse, building, weapon, vehicle, tree, clothing, text, animal.''} $\rightarrow$ the object count behind $A^{\text{object}}$, and the presence flag behind $A^{\text{vlm}}$.
  \item \emph{``Which scene fits best: teaching, family, playing, landscape, drawing?''} $\rightarrow$ the scene category and its confidence.
  \item \emph{``Describe any archival handwriting or text visible.''} $\rightarrow$ used by the Tier 2 OCR agent, not by Tier 1.
  \item \emph{``List relational triplets in (Subject, Predicate, Object) format.''} $\rightarrow$ downstream enrichment only.
\end{enumerate}

\notebox{WarnInk}{\textbf{Why question 1 matters for the whole thesis.} Both $A^{\text{object}}$ and $A^{\text{vlm}}$ are derived from the \emph{same} answer to question~1 --- one as a count, one as a presence flag. That is the mechanical reason $A^{\text{vlm}}=\mathbb{I}[A^{\text{object}}>0]$ holds on all $12{,}110$ images, and therefore the reason the Agreement term collapses (\S2.2a). It is a property of the data path, not of how the models behave.}

\paragraph{Where each number is read from.} The deployed table records, per row, whether a signal
came from its own model output file or from a manifest fallback:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}l l l@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Signal} & \textbf{Recorded source} & \textbf{File the value came from} \\
\midrule
$A^{\text{object}}$ & \texttt{proxy} & manifest column \texttt{vqa\_total\_objects} \\
$A^{\text{scene}}$  & \texttt{proxy} & manifest column \texttt{vqa\_scene\_confidence} \\
$A^{\text{agree}}$  & \texttt{proxy} & computed from the two above \\
$A^{\text{vlm}}$    & \texttt{measured} & \texttt{vqa\_binary\_classification.json} \\
$A^{\text{ocr}}$    & \texttt{measured} & \texttt{kosmos\_grounding.jsonl} \\

\end{center}
\texttt{proxy} here means the value was read from the manifest rather than from a separate
per-model output file; it does not mean the value was invented. The intended object path

fallback fired for all $12{,}110$.

\notebox{GoodInk}{\textbf{What survived a rebuild.} The raw model outputs were retained, so all four signals were later re-derived from them directly --- Grounding DINO detection confidences ($12{,}100$ distinct values against the deployed four), SigLIP re-run corpus-wide ($1{,}520$ against two), a semantic overlap between the VLM's object list and the detector's labels, and OCR substance discounted for repetition. Retested without the circularity, disagreement still did not beat a plain confidence baseline. The finding is a property of the problem, not of the plumbing.}

\section{Fusion and Uncertainty}

\begin{formulacard}{2.1 \quad Scene-Aware Agreement --- the classification score \status{Deployed everywhere}}
SAA$_i$ (a.k.a.\ $S_{\text{fusion}}$) answers ``is a scene present?'' It is a weighted vote, not an uncertainty measure --- that is $U_{\text{SAA}}$, next.
\begin{equation}
SAA_i = w_o \cdot A_i^{\text{object}} + w_s \cdot A_i^{\text{scene}} + w_v \cdot A_i^{\text{vlm}}
\end{equation}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llp{8.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Variable} & \textbf{Value} & \textbf{Meaning} \\
\midrule
$w_o$ & 0.6 & Object weight --- dominant by construction. No tuning or selection produced this value \\
$w_s$ & 0.2 & Scene weight \\
$w_v$ & 0.2 & VLM weight \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Fixed heuristic weights --- not learned, not Shapley-derived. Confirmed byte-identical across the in-sample and clean-holdout scripts, so every reported number in the thesis uses this exact vector. Thresholded at 0.5 to produce a binary scene-present prediction; that binary prediction, compared against $y_{\text{expert}}$, is what defines ``error'' everywhere in the thesis (AWLF's target, CRC's \texttt{error\_flag}).

\derivedfrom{Thesis \S2.4, Level 2}
\computedby{\texttt{run\_agent\_comparison.py} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{comparison\_fusion\_score}}
\par\noindent{\small\textbf{Weights fixed in} \texttt{config.yaml}}
\end{formulacard}

\begin{formulacard}{2.2 \quad $U_{\text{SAA}}$ --- the disagreement / uncertainty proxy \status{Deployed everywhere}}
This is the number behind every headline error-detection figure in the thesis (ROC-AUC 0.9724 in-sample; the corrected group-level holdout split of $0.9687$ on scene-positive images against $0.1259$ on confirmed true negatives; CRC). It is \emph{not} a function of $SAA_i$. The $0.980/0.020$ pair reported in an earlier draft is superseded --- that negative-control pool was $66.7\%$ mislabelled (card 3.4).
\begin{equation}
U_{\text{SAA},i} = \operatorname{std}\!\left( A_i^{\text{object}},\ A_i^{\text{agree}},\ A_i^{\text{scene}},\ A_i^{\text{vlm}} \right)
\end{equation}
Cross-agent standard deviation over four raw per-image signals --- Object, Agreement, Scene, VLM. High when the four disagree with each other; low when they concur (whether concurring correctly or, per the negative-control gap in \S4.3, concurring \emph{incorrectly}).

\notebox{WarnInk}{\textbf{Say this cold if asked:} $U_{\text{SAA},i} \neq 1 - SAA_i$. They share three of four inputs but use different operators (weighted mean vs.\ standard deviation) and different signal sets (SAA has no Agreement term; $U_{\text{SAA}}$ has no independent Scene/VLM-only weighting). An earlier thesis draft defined $U_{\text{SAA}}$ as the algebraic complement of SAA --- that was found wrong during the reproducibility audit and corrected throughout the document (\S2.4, \S4.1, \S7.4).}

\derivedfrom{Thesis \S2.4, Level 3 (corrected)}
\computedby{\texttt{\seqsplit{exp29\_conformal\_risk\_control.py}} as \texttt{sigma\_agents}}
\par\noindent{\small\textbf{Also reproduced in} \texttt{\seqsplit{dual\_coordinator\_orchestrator.py}}}
\par\noindent{\small\textbf{Core agent columns:} \texttt{[existing\_pipeline\_agent, agreement\_agent, scene\_agent, vlm\_agent]}}
\end{formulacard}

\begin{formulacard}{2.2a \quad What $A^{\text{agree}}$ actually reduces to \status{Added Sept 2026}}
The Agreement term is \emph{derived} from two of the four signals, not measured:
\begin{equation}
A_i^{\text{agree}} = 1 - \left|A_i^{\text{object}} - A_i^{\text{vlm}}\right|
\end{equation}
Since $A^{\text{vlm}}\in\{0,1\}$ this has two branches, giving the full grid
\[
A^{\text{agree}} \;=\;
\bordermatrix{
 & A^{\text{vlm}}{=}0 & A^{\text{vlm}}{=}1 \cr
A^{\text{object}}{=}0        & \mathbf{1}    & 0 \cr
A^{\text{object}}{=}\tfrac13 & \tfrac23      & \mathbf{\tfrac13} \cr
A^{\text{object}}{=}\tfrac23 & \tfrac13      & \mathbf{\tfrac23} \cr
A^{\text{object}}{=}1        & 0             & \mathbf{1} \cr}
\]
Because $A^{\text{vlm}}=\mathbb{I}[A^{\text{object}}>0]$ on every image, only the bold cells occur, and the term collapses to
\begin{equation}
A^{\text{agree}} =
\begin{cases}
1 & \text{if } A^{\text{object}} = 0\\
A^{\text{object}} & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}ccc r l@{}}
\toprule
$A^{\text{object}}$ & $A^{\text{vlm}}$ & $A^{\text{agree}}$ & images & relation \\
\midrule
$0$ & $0$ & $1$ & $1{,}403$ & \textbf{jumps to 1} \\
$\tfrac13$ & $1$ & $\tfrac13$ & $2{,}439$ & identical to $A^{\text{object}}$ \\
$\tfrac23$ & $1$ & $\tfrac23$ & $1{,}534$ & identical to $A^{\text{object}}$ \\
$1$ & $1$ & $1$ & $6{,}734$ & identical to $A^{\text{object}}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\notebox{WarnInk}{\textbf{Say this cold if asked:} $A^{\text{agree}}$ is identical to $A^{\text{object}}$ on $10{,}707$ of $12{,}110$ images ($88.4\%$), so the spread is over the object value \emph{counted twice}. On the other $1{,}403$ it inverts discontinuously to $1$, becoming a maximal outlier exactly where the ensemble unanimously reports nothing present.}

\computedby{\texttt{run\_uncertainty\_signal\_decomposition.py} (\texttt{--verify} reproduces)}
\end{formulacard}

\begin{formulacard}{2.2c \quad Two images worked end to end \status{Added Sept 2026}}
\textbf{Unanimous present} --- \texttt{PPN1756550050/00000047\_1}, the largest state ($4{,}154$ images):
\begin{align*}
\mathbf a = (1,1,1,1),\quad \bar a = 1,\quad \textstyle\sum(a_j-\bar a)^2 = 0
\;\Rightarrow\; U_{\text{SAA}} = \sqrt{0/3} = \mathbf{0}\\
SAA = 0.6(1)+0.2(1)+0.2(1) = \mathbf{1.0000},\qquad
U_{\text{triage}} = 0.6(0)+0.4(1-1) = \mathbf{0}
\end{align*}
\textbf{Unanimous absent} --- \texttt{PPN1756555435/00000033\_2} ($1{,}208$ images). Here $A^{\text{object}}=0$, so the derived term jumps to $1$:
\begin{align*}
\mathbf a &= (0,\,1,\,0.5,\,0), \qquad \bar a = 0.375\\
(a_j-\bar a) &= (-0.375,\ +0.625,\ +0.125,\ -0.375)\\
\textstyle\sum(a_j-\bar a)^2 &= 0.140625+\underline{\mathbf{0.390625}}+0.015625+0.140625 = 0.6875\\
U_{\text{SAA}} &= \sqrt{0.6875/3} = \mathbf{0.4787}, \quad SAA = \mathbf{0.1000}, \quad
U_{\text{triage}} = 0.6(0.4787)+0.4(0.625) = \mathbf{0.5372}
\end{align*}
\notebox{WarnInk}{\textbf{The whole argument in one number:} the underlined term is the \emph{derived} one. It supplies $0.390625$ of the $0.6875$ total, while the three \emph{measured} signals $(0,\,0.5,\,0)$ sit close together. The uncertainty here comes from the construction, not from models disagreeing.}

\notebox{GoodInk}{\textbf{Expect to be challenged on image 2, and answer like this.} ``The score reports $0.4787$ --- $83\%$ of the maximum $0.5774$ --- on an image where the ensemble unanimously reports nothing. That is backwards, and it is the construction's fault, not the data's.'' Then take the initiative and give the consequence, because it is the finding: on a true negative an error needs $SAA\ge0.5$, and with $A^{\text{object}}=0$ the score cannot exceed $0.2$. So this whole stratum is \emph{error-free by arithmetic} --- $115$ of the $319$ confirmed negatives, $0$ errors between them --- and it is exactly where $U_{\text{SAA}}$ is highest. Ranges do not overlap: $[0.4787, 0.5774]$ on the guaranteed-correct images against $[0,\,0.3849]$ on the $204$ that carry all $50$ errors. The proxy ranks every guaranteed-correct image above every error before a single model output is consulted. That is where the below-chance $0.1259$ comes from.}
\end{formulacard}

\begin{formulacard}{2.3 \quad $U_{\text{triage}}$ --- two distinct instantiations \status{Fixed 25 Aug 2026}}
This thesis defines two triage scores sharing the same $0.6/0.4$ structural form but built from different second terms. Only one is the quantity Algorithm 1 and the deployed conformal threshold $\hat\lambda$ actually use.

\textbf{(a) SCI-based} (Thesis \S5.1), using the data-driven Spatial Complexity Index $\text{SCI}_i$ (object density / visual clutter, computed independently of agent confidence):
\begin{equation}
U_{\text{triage},i}^{\text{SCI}} = 0.6 \cdot U_{\text{SAA},i} + 0.4 \cdot (1 - \text{SCI}_i)
\end{equation}
This is the tercile-routing policy, reported as a negative result: it performs at chance level (0.98$\times$) on the clean holdout and is \emph{not} deployed.

\textbf{(b) Mean-confidence-based} (``\texttt{unified\_U}''), substituting $\bar{c}_i$ --- the mean of the same four core agents used in $U_{\text{SAA},i}$ --- for $\text{SCI}_i$:
\begin{equation}
U_{\text{triage},i}^{\text{CRC}} = 0.6 \cdot \sigma_{\text{agents},i} + 0.4 \cdot (1 - \bar{c}_i), \qquad \sigma_{\text{agents},i} = U_{\text{SAA},i}
\end{equation}
This is the quantity the deployed Conformal Risk Control policy calibrates and thresholds $\hat\lambda = 0.5370$ against.

\notebox{WarnInk}{\textbf{The distinction that matters:} SCI (scan-level clutter) and $\bar{c}$ (agent-level average confidence) measure different things despite the shared formula shape. An earlier draft presented a single Level-4 formula and let Algorithm 1 imply it fed the deployed threshold --- both the thesis and defense slides now state both variants explicitly (Thesis \S2.4/Algorithm 1/\S5.2; Slides 2.6/3.6).}

\begin{formulacard}{2.10 \quad The scene agent read the wrong SigLIP channel \status{Found Sept 2026}}
SigLIP is trained with a \textbf{sigmoid} objective --- that is what separates it from CLIP. Each image--prompt pair receives an independent calibrated probability, and an image matching none of the prompts scores near zero on all of them. The pipeline discarded that channel and applied a \textbf{softmax} across the five scene prompts:
\begin{equation}
A_i^{\text{scene,deployed}} = \max_c \operatorname{softmax}(z_{ic})
\qquad\text{vs.}\qquad
A_i^{\text{scene,correct}} = \max_c \sigma(z_{ic})
\end{equation}
All five prompts name a \emph{scene type} (teaching, family, playing, landscape, drawing), so a softmax over them is a forced choice \emph{among} scenes and cannot express absence.

\begin{formulacard}{3.1 \quad Platt scaling --- probability calibration \status{Deployed}}
\begin{equation}
P(y=1 \mid SAA_i) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\!\left( A \cdot SAA_i + B \right)}
\end{equation}
A logistic-regression re-calibration of the raw fusion score, fitted on a held-out 50\% split of $C_1$. $A$ and $B$ are the two scalar parameters fit by that regression --- not fixed constants, and not the same $A$ as any agent variable elsewhere in this document (unfortunate but standard notational overlap with the general Platt-scaling literature).
\begin{center}

\begin{formulacard}{3.2 \quad Conformal Risk Control (CRC) \status{Fixed 25 Aug 2026}}
The thesis's actual defended triage mechanism, with a formal distribution-free guarantee. Two thresholds exist and must not be confused: $\hat\lambda = 0.372$ is the \textbf{deployed} value on the corrected group-level pool, and every workload claim rests on it; $\hat\lambda = 0.5370$ belongs to the in-sample $C_1$ calibration and is reported for continuity only.
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{R}(\lambda) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\, \text{error}_i \cdot \mathbb{I}\!\left( U_{\text{triage},i} < \lambda \right) \right] \ \le\ \alpha, \qquad \alpha = 0.05
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
\text{error}_i &= \mathbb{I}\!\left( \text{sign}(S_{\text{fusion},i} \ge 0.5) \neq y_{\text{expert},i} \right) \\
U_{\text{triage},i} &= 0.6 \cdot \sigma_{\text{agents},i} + 0.4 \cdot (1 - \bar{c}_i) \qquad \text{(\S2.3(b) above)}
\end{align}
Two steps, in order: (1) fix the ground-truth error label from the classification score; (2) threshold a \emph{separate} uncertainty score for auto-approval eligibility. Approve when the system is confident enough in its decision, not merely on the decision's raw score.

\notebox{WarnInk}{\textbf{Documented vs.\ implemented, before the fix:} an earlier draft wrote this as directly thresholding $SAA_i$ against $\lambda$ (i.e.\ approve if $SAA_i \ge \lambda$) --- a different, simpler mechanism than what the script does. Corrected 25 Aug 2026 across Thesis \S5.2 and Algorithm 3 to state the real two-step mechanism above, matching \texttt{\seqsplit{exp29\_conformal\_risk\_control.py}} exactly.}

Threshold selection rule (matches the code exactly, verified line-for-line):
\begin{equation}
\hat\lambda = \max\left\{ \lambda \in [0,1] \ :\ \frac{n_{\text{cal}}}{n_{\text{cal}}+1}\, \hat{R}(\lambda) + \frac{1}{n_{\text{cal}}+1} \ \le\ \alpha \right\}
\end{equation}

\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{How $\hat\lambda$ is actually selected.} Take the largest threshold whose finite-sample-corrected empirical risk still clears $\alpha$:
\begin{equation}
\hat\lambda = \max\left\{\lambda \;:\; \frac{n}{n+1}\,\hat R(\lambda) + \frac{1}{n+1} \;\le\; \alpha\right\}
\end{equation}
The $\tfrac{1}{n+1}$ term is what makes the bound hold with no distributional assumption. Worked on the corrected pool, $n=252$, $\alpha=0.05$:
\begin{align*}
\tfrac{1}{n+1} &= \tfrac{1}{253} = 0.003953\\
\tfrac{252}{253}\hat R(\lambda) + 0.003953 \le 0.05
&\iff \hat R(\lambda) \le (0.05-0.003953)\cdot\tfrac{253}{252} = 0.046230\\
\Rightarrow\ \hat\lambda &= 0.372
\end{align*}
On the test partition: $60.6\%$ auto-approval, $91.1\%$ error recall, $\mathcal R = 0.0201 \le 0.05$, efficiency lift $0.911/0.394 \approx 2.31\times$.
\notebox{GoodInk}{\textbf{Score-agnostic.} CRC bounds risk over whatever score it is calibrated on. The findings about what $U_{\text{SAA}}$ measures (\S2.2a) therefore do not weaken this guarantee --- a point worth making explicitly if the disagreement result comes up.}

\end{formulacard}

\begin{formulacard}{4.3 \quad Effective rank --- the heterogeneity precondition \status{Added Sept 2026}}
A label-free check of whether an ensemble still carries enough independent variation for a disagreement statistic over it to mean anything. For the signal covariance $\Sigma$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_i$ and $p_i = \lambda_i / \sum_j \lambda_j$:
\begin{equation}
\text{eff-rank}(\Sigma) = \exp\!\left(-\sum_i p_i \log p_i\right)
\end{equation}
Worked on $C_0$ over the four core signals:
\begin{align*}
\boldsymbol\lambda &= (0.000000,\ 0.043022,\ 0.105629,\ 0.221285)\\
\mathbf p &= (0,\ 0.1163,\ 0.2855,\ 0.5982)\\
H = -\textstyle\sum p_i \log p_i &= 0.9155
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{eff-rank} = e^{0.9155} = \mathbf{2.498}
\end{align*}
\notebox{WarnInk}{\textbf{The first eigenvalue is exactly zero.} Four signals but three free parameters, so $\Sigma$ is singular --- the derived-term dependency of \S2.2a reappearing as a rank deficiency. Four signals carry the information of about two and a half.}
On the second archive the same computation gives $\mathbf{1.37}$ against $2.50$ here: the ensemble collapses toward a single effective signal, which is why disagreement carries no information there. Runs \emph{before} any annotation.

\computedby{\texttt{run\_uncertainty\_signal\_decomposition.py}}
\end{formulacard}

\section{Defense-Critical: Where the Write-Up and the Code Used to Disagree}
Both gaps below were found by reading the actual scripts against the thesis text, then corrected the same day (25 Aug 2026) in \emph{thesis\_v3\_updated.tex} and \emph{defense\_slides.tex} (Algorithm 1, Algorithm 3, \S2.4 Level 4, \S5.2, Slides 2.6 and 3.6) --- rebuilt and visually verified, no overflow or truncation. Neither was a fabrication; both were honest, defensible design choices that simply weren't written down. Keep the explanations below in your head anyway --- a committee member may still ask you to walk through \emph{why} the code does what it does, even though the PDF now states it correctly.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item \textbf{$U_{\text{triage}}$'s second term: SCI vs.\ $\bar{c}$ (mean confidence).} The thesis now documents both as separate, named quantities (\S2.3 above): $U^{\text{SCI}}_{\text{triage}}$ (the non-deployed \S5.1 policy, shown honestly to fail at 0.98$\times$ chance) and $U^{\text{CRC}}_{\text{triage}}$ (substituting $\bar{c}$, mean of the 4 core agents, for SCI --- the quantity $\hat\lambda=0.5370$ is actually calibrated against). \emph{If asked:} these are genuinely different signals sharing a formula shape --- SCI is scan-level clutter, $\bar{c}$ is agent-level average confidence --- and the algorithm/section that previously conflated them now spells out which is which.
\item \textbf{CRC's loss function: thresholding SAA vs.\ thresholding uncertainty.} \S5.2 and Algorithm 3 now state the real two-step mechanism exactly: fix the error label from $SAA \ge 0.5$ first, then threshold the separate $U_{\text{triage}}$ (CRC variant) score for auto-approval --- ``approve when confident enough,'' not ``approve when SAA says so.'' \emph{If asked why:} this is the more defensible design of the two (approving based on confidence-in-the-decision rather than the decision's raw score) --- the fix was to the documentation, not the mechanism, which was always correct and produced every real number in the thesis.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Withdrawn, and why --- have this ready.} An earlier draft carried a proposition on \emph{fixed-point convergence of consensus refinement}: if the iterative consensus operator were a contraction ($\gamma<1$), Banach's theorem would guarantee a unique stationary state. It was removed from the thesis and is no longer claimed anywhere. The reason is that $\gamma<1$ was never proven for the actual kernel-smoothing operator; all that could be reported was that refinement converged within $\le 3$ iterations in every run, which is consistent with contractive behaviour but is not evidence of it. A theorem whose hypothesis is unverified proves nothing about the system, so it was cut rather than left standing as decoration. \emph{If a committee member has an older draft and asks:} say it was withdrawn deliberately, and that the empirical convergence observation survives without it.

\noindent\textbf{Already fixed earlier in the same audit, for reference:} $U_{\text{SAA}} \neq 1-SAA$. An earlier draft defined $U_{\text{SAA}}$ as the algebraic complement of SAA. Found wrong and corrected in three places (\S2.4, \S4.1, \S7.4) to the real 4-term standard-deviation formula, before the two gaps above were even found. If a committee member has seen an older draft, this is the one place text could plausibly still disagree with what is in their hands --- the current version is consistent throughout.
