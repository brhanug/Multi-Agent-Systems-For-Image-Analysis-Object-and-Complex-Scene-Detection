Part 2 of 5 · The first design and why it failed

How were these four numbers made? All four came from the describer’s answer about the picture. A-object counts how many objects it listed and divides by three, with a maximum of one. So zero objects give 0, one object gives one third, two give two thirds, and three or more give 1. Why three? That rule was fixed when the system was built — three or more objects counted as full evidence; I did not choose it, I audited it. A-scene is the describer’s own confidence that the picture shows a scene, and in practice it was only ever 0.5 or 1. A-vlm is 1 if the describer listed any object at all, otherwise 0. And A-agree is not measured: it is computed from A-object and A-vlm. For example, if the describer says “a man and a horse”, that is two objects, so A-object is two thirds, A-vlm is 1, and A-agree is two thirds. The audit found that, despite their names, all four numbers came from that one model.
