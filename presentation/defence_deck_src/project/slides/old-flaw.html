Part 2 of 5 · The first design and why it failed

Three problems sink the old score. First, the archive has only eight states, so no formula can rank pictures finely; I checked every weighting with linear programming, and 0.6/0.2/0.2 is already the best of the six possible decision rules. Second, the score’s good-looking AUROC of 0.979 comes from the computed Agreement term; without it, it drops to 0.75, and plain confidence — no disagreement at all — reaches 0.978. Third, when every model agrees there is nothing in the picture, the derived term makes the “disagreement” score almost maximal. So the old score was a confidence score in disguise, and a bad one. The corrected design measures each agent properly and uses confidence openly.
