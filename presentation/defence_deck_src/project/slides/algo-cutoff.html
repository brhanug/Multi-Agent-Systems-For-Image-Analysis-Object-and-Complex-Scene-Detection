Algorithm 3 · done once, on the calibration books

Choosing the cut-off (conformal risk control)

INPUT: n calibration pictures, each with its uncertainty u and whether its answer was wrong (e = 1) or right (e = 0); the promise α

1 for each candidate cut-off λ from 0.001 to 1:

2 rate(λ) = share of pictures with u < λ and e = 1

(would be approved, and are wrong)

3 keep the largest λ with

(n ÷ (n+1)) · rate(λ) + 1 ÷ (n+1) ≤ α

4 if no λ qualifies, approve nothing

OUTPUT: the cut-off λ̂, one per answer

In one sentence: approve as much as possible while the labelled examples show the promise is kept, with a small safety margin.

WORKED OUT, α = 5%

n = 252 → at most 11 approved and wrong

λ̂ = 0.829 → approve if p is more than 0.171 from 0.5

test: 70.7% approved, real risk 0.036

Part 3 of 5 · The corrected design · the algorithms

Algorithm three chooses the cut-off, once, on the calibration books. Each calibration picture has an uncertainty and we know whether the system’s answer was wrong. For every possible cut-off, count the share of pictures that would be approved — uncertainty below the cut-off — and are wrong. Keep the largest cut-off whose share, with the small correction n over n plus one and the extra one over n plus one, stays at or below alpha. That correction is what makes the promise hold for new pictures, not just the calibration ones. If no cut-off works, nothing is approved. In one sentence: approve as much as possible while the labelled examples show the promise is kept, with a safety margin. With 252 calibration pictures and alpha five percent, at most 11 may be approved and wrong; the cut-off is 0.829, and on the test pictures 70.7 percent are approved with a real risk of 3.6 percent.
