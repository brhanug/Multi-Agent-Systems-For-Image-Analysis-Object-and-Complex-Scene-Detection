Step ③ · from a report to a probability

A small learned mapping per agent

p = 1 ÷ (1 + e−(bias + Σ weight × score))

A logistic regression, fitted on the training books only.

Weights say how much each score counts as evidence — learned, never set by hand.

Bias is the answer when the agent sees nothing: 84.5% of training pictures are scenes, so “nothing seen” still leans to “scene”.

Example: grandmother and boy

Object finder sees person 0.972, animal 0.013:
sum 3.86 → p = 0.98

Scene matcher: its bias 2.01 plus family 0.327 and drawing 0.157 (sum 2.72) → p = 0.94

Describer: person 0.989, child 0.995, animal 0.311; sum 4.55 → p = 0.99

Part 3 of 5 · The corrected design

Step three. The three reports are on different scales and mean different things, so each is mapped to the same quantity: a probability. The mapping is a logistic regression, learned on the training books only. Multiply each score by a learned weight, add a bias, and squash with the logistic function into a number between 0 and 1. The weights say how much each class counts as evidence — for example a person counts strongly towards “scene”. The bias is what the agent says when it sees nothing; since 84.5 percent of the training pictures are scenes, even “nothing seen” leans towards scene. On the right is a real picture, an old woman talking to a boy: the object finder gives 0.98, the scene matcher, whose family and drawing sentences fit, gives 0.94, and the describer gives 0.99. For the scene classes the same mapping has six outputs instead of one.
