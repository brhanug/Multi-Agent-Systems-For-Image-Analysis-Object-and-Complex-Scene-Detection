Result 6 · why the first two rows fail

The promise is only as good as the calibration pictures

Cause 1 · two meanings of “scene”

The pool’s labels called ornaments, borders and sheet music “drawing” — a scene. On archive pictures labelled under one rule, the scene calls were wrong on 37.8%.

Learning under one rule: 37.8% → 11.0% wrong, risk 16.2% → 8.5%.

Cause 2 · the pool is not a random sample

The promise assumes calibration and new pictures come from the same mix. The pool was assembled, not drawn, so its cut-off was too generous: 94.2% approved on the archive.

Calibrating on random archive pictures: 8.5% → 5.4–5.9%.

Practical rule: label 200–400 random pictures of your own collection under the definition you mean to use, and set the cut-off on them. Then about four pictures in five are approved, at a risk just above 5% (5.4–5.9%).

Part 4 of 5 · Results

Why did the first two rows fail? Two causes. First, the labels mixed two meanings of a scene: in the pool, ornaments, borders and sheet music were labelled “drawing”, which counts as a scene. On archive pictures labelled under one rule, the system’s scene calls were wrong on 37.8 percent. Learning under one rule brings that to 11 percent, and the risk from 16.2 to 8.5. Second, the promise rests on one assumption: the calibration pictures and the new pictures come from the same mix. The pool was assembled, not drawn at random, so its cut-off was too generous — it approved 94 percent of archive pictures. Calibrating on random archive pictures fixes most of this and brings the risk to 5.4 to 5.9 percent — just above five. So the practical rule for any archive: label two to four hundred random pictures of your own collection under the definition you mean to use, and set the cut-off on them.
