Step ⑥ · the risk promise (conformal risk control)

“At most 5% of all pictures approved and wrong”

1. Take calibration pictures with known labels, never used for training.

2. For each possible cut-off λ, count how many would be approved and wrong (u < λ and the answer is wrong); u = 1 − |p − 0.5| says how unsure it is.

3. Keep the largest λ whose rate, with a small safety allowance, stays ≤ α:
(n ÷ (n+1)) · rate(λ) + 1 ÷ (n+1) ≤ α

4. New picture: approve if u < λ̂, otherwise a person checks.

Worked out, α = 5%

n = 252 calibration pictures → allowance 1/253

→ at most 11 of 252 may be approved and wrong

→ λ̂ = 0.829: approve if p is more than 0.171 from 0.5

→ on 249 test pictures: 70.7% approved, real risk 0.036

The example is 0.47 from 0.5 → approved.

The one condition: calibration pictures must be like the pictures it is used on. The archive test checks exactly this.

Part 3 of 5 · The corrected design

Step six is the risk promise, conformal risk control. The promise is: on average, at most five percent of all pictures are approved automatically and wrong. How it works: take calibration pictures that have human labels and were not used for training. For every possible cut-off, count how many of them would be approved and wrong. Pick the largest cut-off that keeps that rate, plus a small safety allowance of one over n plus one, at or below alpha. Then a new picture is approved if its uncertainty is below that cut-off. On the right is the real calculation: 252 calibration pictures means at most 11 of them may be approved and wrong. The chosen cut-off is 0.829, which means a picture is approved if its probability is more than 0.171 away from 0.5. On 249 test pictures that approves 70.7 percent with a real risk of 3.6 percent, inside the promise. The example is 0.47 away, so it is approved. The one condition is that calibration pictures look like the pictures it is used on — we will see that this matters on the archive. For objects the same method promises a different thing: that at most a set share of a picture’s objects is missing from its list.
