Old design · the object detector

A detector graded by its teacher looks good — and is not

0.768

scored against its teacher’s labels (mAP50)

0.146

the same detector against human boxes

Why

The teacher never labelled child, building, weapon, vehicle or text; 88.4% of its boxes were persons. Of 128 objects the student missed, 126 were never in the teacher’s labels.

Fix

A better teacher (Grounding DINO) raises the student from 0.123 to 0.210 mean AP50 against human boxes (my own, stricter evaluator, which gives the old detector 0.123 where the library’s gives 0.146).

Lesson: a model trained on another model’s labels must be graded against people.

Part 2 of 5 · The first design and why it failed

The second problem was the object detector. It was trained on labels made by a larger “teacher” model. Graded against the teacher’s labels it scored 0.768, which looks good. Graded against boxes I drew by hand on 300 pictures, the same detector scored 0.146. The reason is simple: the teacher never labelled children, buildings, weapons, vehicles or text, so the student never learned them, and the teacher-based score could not notice. Of 128 objects the student missed, 126 were never in the teacher’s labels. Both of those numbers come from the detector library’s own validator. To compare detectors I use my own, stricter evaluator, which gives the old detector 0.123 on the same boxes; with a better teacher, Grounding DINO, the retrained student goes from 0.123 to 0.210. The lesson: always grade against people. With these problems found, the next part turns each of them into a design decision.
