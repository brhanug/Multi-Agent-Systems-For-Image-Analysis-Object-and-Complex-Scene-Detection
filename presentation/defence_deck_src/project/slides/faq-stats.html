A strict rule for calling a difference real

1

Split the labelled books 60 / 20 / 20 into training, calibration and test — 200 times, no book on two sides.

2

In each split, measure both designs on the same test pictures and take the difference.

3

Established = the middle 95% of the 200 differences lies entirely on one side of zero.

This is stricter than the standard corrected test (Nadeau & Bengio): all 23 established differences pass it too. Key tests were written down before they were run.

Backup C

What does “established” mean? I split the labelled books into training, calibration and test 200 times, never putting a book on two sides. In each split, both designs are measured on the same test pictures, and I take the difference. I call it established only if the middle 95 percent of those 200 differences is entirely on one side of zero. Because the splits share pictures, I also checked the standard corrected resampled t-test of Nadeau and Bengio; my rule is the stricter one, and every difference I call established also passes that test. The key tests were written down before they were run, so the results could not be tuned to them.
