Old design · levels 2 to 4

From four numbers to “approve or check”

Level 2 · decide: SAA = 0.6·A-object + 0.2·A-scene + 0.2·A-vlm. Scene if SAA ≥ 0.5.

Level 3 · disagreement: U-SAA = standard deviation of the four signals.

Level 4 · route: U-triage = 0.6·U-SAA + 0.4·(1 − mean of the four). Approve if U-triage < λ̂.

One example picture

A-object = ⅔, A-scene = 1, A-vlm = 1
A-agree = 1 − |⅔ − 1| = ⅔

SAA = 0.6·⅔ + 0.2 + 0.2 = 0.80 → scene

spread = 0.19, mean = 0.83
U-triage = 0.6·0.19 + 0.4·0.17 = 0.18

λ̂ = 0.372 at a 5% promise → 0.18 < 0.372 → approved

All weights (0.6/0.2/0.2 and 0.6/0.4) were set by hand. Learning them later changed nothing that matters (backup slide).

Part 2 of 5 · The first design and why it failed

Here are the three formulas, with one example picture on the right. Level two decides: a weighted average with weights 0.6, 0.2, 0.2; at 0.5 or above it is a scene. Level three measures disagreement: the standard deviation of the four numbers. Level four routes the picture: 0.6 times the disagreement plus 0.4 times one minus the average. If that is below the cut-off lambda-hat, the picture is approved. In the example, the picture has two-thirds object signal and full scene and VLM signals. SAA is 0.8, so it is a scene. Disagreement is 0.19, the average 0.83, so the routing score is 0.18. The cut-off chosen by conformal risk control at a five percent promise is 0.372, so it is approved. All these weights were chosen by hand; I later learned them from data and it changed nothing important.
