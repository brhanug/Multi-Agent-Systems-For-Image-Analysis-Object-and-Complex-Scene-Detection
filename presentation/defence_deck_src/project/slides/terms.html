Nine words you will hear

The vocabulary of this talk, in plain words

Agent — one model that looks at the picture on its own and reports numbers; it never sees the other agents.

Zero-shot — a model used as it comes, with no training on this archive.

Logistic regression — a simple formula that turns numbers into a probability; its weights are learned from labelled examples.

Confidence — how far a probability is from a coin flip: 0.5 = unsure, near 0 or 1 = sure.

AUROC — how well a score puts the right pictures first: 0.5 = coin flip, 1.0 = perfect order.

Balanced accuracy — the share correct, averaged so that every class counts equally, rare or common.

Risk promise α — “on average at most α of all pictures are approved automatically and wrong”, e.g. 5%.

Calibration pictures — labelled pictures kept apart, used only to set the approval cut-off λ̂.

Book-disjoint split — training, calibration and test pictures come from different books; redrawn 200 times.

Part 1 of 5 · The question

Nine words come up again and again. An agent is one model looking at a picture on its own. Zero-shot means a model used as it comes, without training on this archive. A logistic regression is a simple formula that turns numbers into a probability, with weights learned from labelled examples. Confidence is how far a probability is from a coin flip. AUROC measures how well a score puts the right pictures first — 0.5 is a coin flip, 1 is perfect. Balanced accuracy is the share correct, averaged so that rare classes count as much as common ones. The risk promise alpha is the guarantee: on average at most that share of all pictures is approved and wrong. Calibration pictures are labelled pictures kept apart only to set the cut-off. And book-disjoint means training, calibration and test pictures come from different books, and this split is redrawn 200 times.
