The answer in one slide

Three findings

1

Disagreement between models warns of mistakes no better than confidence. In a test fixed in advance: 0.378 against 0.825 (AUROC); within each task 0.78–0.82 against 0.83–0.84.

2

A corrected design routes by confidence under a risk promise. It approves 76.4% of pictures at a 5% promise (old design: 63.0%) and ranks the 8 object classes at 0.903.

3

The promise holds on the archive only if calibrated on the archive. Calibrated on the labelled pool: 16.2% approved and wrong. Calibrated on random archive pictures: 5.4–5.9%, just above the 5%.

Part 1 of 5 · The question

Here is the whole thesis in three sentences. One: the system was built on the idea that when several models disagree, the answer is doubtful. That idea does not hold — disagreement never warns better than the system’s own confidence. Two: so I built a corrected design that uses confidence, with a statistical risk promise; it lets more pictures through automatically than the old design. Three: the promise only holds on the real archive if it is calibrated on pictures drawn from that archive. The rest of the talk explains each of these.
