Part 2 of 5 · The first design and why it failed

Why build a system with several agents at all? It started from two observations. First, one detector taken off the shelf is weak on old prints: 0.79, 0.74 and 0.75 AUROC for person, animal and vehicle. Second, when we trained a detector on another model’s labels, it looked good by its own measure, 0.768, but was poor against people, 0.146 — and nothing inside the pipeline noticed. A single model has no independent signal to check itself against. So the idea was: use several models that share neither architecture nor training data, and where they disagree, let a person look. That gave the first design its three parts: independent agents, a measure of disagreement, and a risk promise. The middle part, disagreement, is the hypothesis this thesis tests.
