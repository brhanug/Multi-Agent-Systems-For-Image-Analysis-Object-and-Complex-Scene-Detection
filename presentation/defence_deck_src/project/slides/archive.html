Part 4 of 5 · Results

Everything so far was measured inside the labelled pool. The real test is the archive. I drew pictures at random from all 12,110, labelled them myself, and ran the system. The test passes if at most five percent of these pictures are approved automatically and wrong. Row one: with the cut-off set on the pool, as in all results so far, 16.2 percent of 399 random pictures — about 65 — were approved and wrong. That is more than three times the promise, in every one of the redraws, so the verdict is fail. Row two: with one consistent definition of a scene it falls to 8.5 percent, still 1.7 times too much — fail. Row three: one definition, and the cut-off set on the 399 random archive pictures, then tested on 294 new pictures with the plan written in advance. By the planned measure it is 5.9 percent; with the calibration set as drawn, 5.4, with a range from 3.1 to 8.2. Just over five, within the chance variation of a test this size — I report it honestly as narrowly missed. Row four: at a stricter 2.5 percent setting the risk is 4.6 percent — below five, but not the 2.5 promised. Note that the ranking was good throughout: what went wrong was where the cut-off sat, not which pictures the system trusted.
