Part 5 of 5 · What it means

Here I separate what is proven from what is only likely and what is not shown. Proven, meaning the whole 95 percent range is on one side of zero: confidence beats disagreement in the registered test and on the object labels, and on scene presence the two tie; the corrected design approves 13.5 and 26.1 points more than the old system; objects are ranked far better than by the old detector and better than by one model with a linear classifier; a better teacher makes a better detector; and calibrating on archive pictures cuts the risk from 16.2 to 5.4–5.9 percent, just above the promised level. Likely but not proven: a better ranking of scenes, and the learned combination being slightly better than the describer alone. Not shown: better scene decisions themselves, better scene classes than the old system or one model, automatic confirmation of objects, and three agents clearly beating one. I want to be exact about this, because the thesis is about trusting answers.
