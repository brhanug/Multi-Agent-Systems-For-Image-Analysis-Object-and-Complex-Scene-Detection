\section{The argument, in eight steps}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=2pt, topsep=3pt]
  \item A COCO-trained detector applied as-is is weak on these scans: AUROC $0.79$ / $0.74$ / $0.75$ for person / animal / vehicle.
  \item A student trained on its teacher's pseudo-labels scores $\text{mAP}_{50}=\num{0.768}$ against those labels and \num{0.146} against human boxes. 126 of 128 objects it misses were never in the teacher's labels; a better teacher raises it to \num{0.210}.
  \item The system was built on the hypothesis that \emph{disagreement} between its agents signals doubt. As deployed, its four agent signals came from two measurements: the whole archive fell into eight states.
  \item In a test fixed in writing before it was run, disagreement ranks the system's errors at \num{0.378}, plain confidence at \num{0.825} (300 hand-labelled pictures). The errors lie where the agents \emph{agree}.
  \item Agreement also fails as a filter for training labels: students trained on all, confirmed or randomly thinned pseudo-labels score \num{0.214} / \num{0.232} / \num{0.201}, and an earlier run with the same seeds ordered them the other way.
  \item A corrected design --- every agent reports what it measures, confidence decides what a person checks --- approves \num{76.4\%} of pictures automatically at a promised risk of $5\%$, against \num{63.0\%} for the old triage score (\num{61.1\%} routing by disagreement alone), on 199 of 200 book-disjoint redraws of the labelled pool. On 400 pictures drawn at random from the archive that promise fails when the cut-off is calibrated on the pool ($16.2\%$ approved and wrong); with one definition of a scene and a cut-off calibrated on archive pictures it comes to just above the promised level: $5.4$--$5.9\%$ at $80\%$ approved on 300 new pictures; set to $2.5\%$, the promise gives $4.6\%$, so it is missed too. Inside the pool every approval is a confident ``scene'' call, and the scene / no-scene decision itself is no better than before.
  \item On the fixed classes: the agents rank the \textbf{8 object classes} well (mean AUROC \num{0.903}) and a promise on missed objects gives each picture a list of $2.8$ classes missing $9.2\%$ of a picture's objects on average, as promised ($10.8\%$ of all objects). For the \textbf{5 scene classes} they shortlist but do not decide: the combination must be learned ($0.401 \to 0.544$ balanced accuracy), which finds family, playing and landscape in $27$--$62\%$ of cases; under a $5\%$ promise only ``no scene'' and ``drawing'' are approved reliably enough.
  \item \textbf{The contribution is therefore not a better detector} but a tested account of when an automatic answer can be trusted, with a risk promise attached.
\end{enumerate}

\section{What this thesis does \emph{not} claim}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Not that disagreement measures uncertainty.} On this archive it does not; confidence does better.
  \item \textbf{Not a guarantee for every batch.} The risk promise holds on average, for books like the calibration books.
  \item \textbf{Not that the accuracy gains transfer.} With labels from a second source the old design ranks better; the two label sources agree only at $\kappa=0.41$.
  \item \textbf{Not automatic objects or scene classes.} A promise on all decisions confirms at most $0.4\%$ of objects, and only ``no scene'' and ``drawing'' are approved among the scene classes; boxes reach a mean AP50 of $0.210$.
  \item \textbf{Not that several agents clearly beat one.} Learned, the combination is barely ahead of LLaVA alone.
\end{itemize}

\section{People, labels and the blind spot}
\textbf{People agree only moderately.} Two raters agree on scene type at $\kappa=0.337$ (124 pictures); the same rater, rating 143 pictures a second time, reaches only $\kappa=0.36$ against the first rating. Most disagreement is about ``drawing'': counting it as ``no scene'' lifts these to $0.50$ and $0.58$. Scene categories are interpretations, not facts.

\textbf{The shared blind spot.} Mistakes are six times more frequent on the sparsest pictures ($30.0\%$ against $5.1\%$ by ink quartile), and on 149 of the 199 mistakes all three agents are wrong as well. Confidence already captures this; six picture properties add only $+0.006$, overall and among auto-approved pictures, with intervals spanning zero.

\section{Reproducibility}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Every experiment script carries a \texttt{-{}-verify} flag that recomputes its result and fails if it has drifted. The whole pipeline was rerun twice from scratch.
  \item Three checks hold the thesis text to the result files: \num{39}, \num{444} and \num{50} figures, all matching. The class tasks were planned in writing before they were run.
\end{itemize}

\section{Known limitations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The evaluation rests on few labelled pictures: 249 test pictures, of which 38 show no scene; the class ``teaching'' has only 6 labelled pictures.
  \item The archive tests and the re-audits rest on one person's labels; the four specific scene classes rest on 165 expert pictures.
  \item Tier 3 (paid frontier models) is designed but was not run.
  \item The Europeana transfer pilot ($n=24$) misses $65.2\%$ of confirmed scenes: the method does not transfer unchanged.
\end{itemize}
