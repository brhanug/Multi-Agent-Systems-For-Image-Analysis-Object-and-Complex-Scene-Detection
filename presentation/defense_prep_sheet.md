## 🔴 The question you will definitely get: the data-integrity correction

**"Your thesis says the guarantee failed, then says it holds. Which is it?"**

> "It holds. The first result was wrong because the *evaluation data* was wrong, not
> the method. 1,199 of the 2,000 images in that pool carried catalog-default 'no
> scene' labels that had never been through human review. Two independent raters
> re-checked 449 of them and found 57.5% mislabeled — real scenes marked absent.
> Rebuilt on a corrected 1,250-image pool with 196 confirmed negatives, the risk
> bound holds at every α tested: R = 0.0201 at α = 0.05, 60.6% auto-approval at
> 91.1% error recall, a 2.31× efficiency lift."

**"Your lift estimate moved from 7.10× to 3.09×. Why should I trust the new one?"**
*(The sharpest version of this question. Answer it head-on.)*

> "Because it moved for a reason I can explain, and in the direction you'd expect.
> The 7.10× figure came from an intermediate correction with only **7** confirmed
> negatives in the test partition. When the negative sample grew to **38**, the
> estimate settled at 2.31–3.09×. Small negative-control samples inflate lift — that
> is exactly the caution the thesis raises. I report the revision openly rather than
> only the final number, because the pattern is the finding."

**"How do I know the re-labelling isn't just your opinion?"**

> "Two independent raters, zero image overlap between their passes. The first found
> 66.7% mislabeled on 144 images; the second found 53.1% on a disjoint 305. They
> converged without seeing each other's work — 57.5% combined. That agreement is
> the evidence, not either rater alone."

**"What if the remaining unreviewed images change the answer again?"**

> "750 of the 1,199 are still unreviewed, and I say so in the Limitations section.
> Based on two independent raters agreeing so far, I'd expect the lift to moderate
> slightly further and the negative-control finding to get *more* credible, not to
> reverse. But it is an open number, and I'm not claiming otherwise."

## 🧠 Core method

**"What exactly is your uncertainty metric?"** *(They will try to catch you here.)*

> "Three distinct quantities, defined at Levels 2–4 of the methodology. **$SAA_i$**
> is the classification fusion score: 0.6·Object + 0.2·Scene + 0.2·VLM. **$U_{SAA,i}$**
> is the uncertainty proxy: the standard deviation across Object, Agreement, Scene
> and VLM. **$U_{\text{triage},i}$** is the operational routing score:
> 0.6·$U_{SAA}$ + 0.4·(1 − mean confidence). They are related but not
> algebraically equivalent — $U_{SAA} \neq 1 - SAA$, and the thesis says so explicitly."

**"Isn't disagreement just a proxy? You're not measuring uncertainty."**

> "Correct, and I claim exactly that. It's an operational proxy justified by
> predictive utility, not a Bayesian posterior. The thesis's 'Does NOT Claim'
> section states it in those words."

**"A single model does nearly as well — 0.9726 vs your 0.9724. What's the contribution?"**

> "Not discrimination — we tie there, and I say so. The contribution is
> **calibration**: ECE below 0.001 after Platt scaling, against roughly 0.19 for
> that baseline. Two orders of magnitude. A tied AUROC with wildly different
> calibration means one of them can be thresholded safely and the other can't."

**"Why not fine-tune a VLM end-to-end?"**

> "Annotation scarcity and latency. There aren't thousands of expert boxes for this
> archive, and the 7B VLM runs at ~15 s/image against 0.02 s for the distilled
> detector — a factor of ~750. The escalation ladder exists so the expensive models
> only see the fraction that needs them."

**"Why don't the Tier 2 agents feed back into the trust signal?"**

> "The Architectural Decoupling Rule. Tier 2 consumes post-validation metadata. If
> Agent 2's contradictions fed $SAA$, the uncertainty signal would be validated by
> decisions derived from the very predictions it is supposed to audit — circular."

## 📊 Results

**"Explain the 0.989 → 0.1462 collapse."**

> "Same model, two ground truths. Graded against its own teacher's pseudo-labels it
> scores 0.989; graded against a human on 300 images it scores 0.1462. The student
> learned to reproduce the teacher's hallucinations. That's the observation that
> motivates the whole thesis — a model cannot grade its own homework."

**"P@10 = 1.000 looks too good."**

> "It is a 10-query pilot, and the relevance labels are a proxy that shares agent
> scores with the retrieval signal — so it is supplementary evidence, not an
> unbiased benchmark. I state that in the thesis and on the slide."

**"Your Tier 2 agents have poor precision. Why keep them?"**

> "Because I measured them and reported it. Agent 2 is 23.3% precise as a
> hallucination detector on 101 gold-labelled candidates — it mostly catches YOLO
> misses, so 'cross-modal disagreement worth review' is the honest description.
> Agent 4 is 55.7% overall but 100% above similarity 0.92, which gives a deployable
> threshold. Agent 5's 'real OCR' bucket is 52.5% — near a coin flip. None of them
> feed the headline results, and all three now have real numbers instead of
> spot-checks."

**"What does the Tier 1 ablation show?"**

> "Removing grounded OCR causes the largest drop in disagreement, −0.0457, because
> OCR is near-uncorrelated with the other three (r ≤ 0.07) and contributes the most
> independent variance. Removing Spatial or VQA — the most correlated pair at
> r = 0.70 — slightly *increases* disagreement."

## ⚠️ Limitations (volunteer these before they're extracted)

**"Where does your method fail?"**

> "Two places, both documented. First, the shared blind spot: on 38 confirmed true
> negatives, disagreement is uninformative — ROC-AUC 0.1152, well below chance.
> When all four models confidently agree on the same mistake, a disagreement signal
> is structurally blind to it. Second, transfer."

**"Does it transfer to another archive?"**

> "Not as-is, and I can tell you exactly why. We labelled 23 of 24 images pulled
> live from the Europeana API. All are scene-positive; the unchanged pipeline misses
> 65.2% of them. But the more useful finding is that I *couldn't* score the
> uncertainty signal there at all: three of the four agents went near-constant — the
> VLM returned only two distinct values across 24 images — so the fusion score
> correlated 0.99 with YOLO alone. A disagreement method needs the ensemble to stay
> heterogeneous on the new domain. That's a precondition you should check before
> claiming transfer, and it's a contribution in its own right."

**"Your validation set is 77.9% drawings."**

> "That's the archive's true long-tail distribution, and drawings are the most
> degraded and ambiguous subset — the hardest testbed, not the easiest. I flag the
> imbalance in Limitations and don't claim per-category generalization."

**"Single annotator?"**

> "Primarily, yes. There is a genuine inter-annotator measurement on the 124 images
> where C1 and C2 overlap: κ = 0.337, 'fair', with 83.9% raw agreement, and the
> disagreement concentrates in *drawing* vs 'no scene' — real category-boundary
> ambiguity. It's a small, incidental sample with no true negatives in it, so I
> present it as an honest limitation and propose a purpose-built double-annotation
> pass."

**"Is restoration inventing historical content?"**

> "Possibly — which is exactly why the restored image is never archival evidence.
> The Axiom of Archival Integrity: $I_{\text{refined}}$ is an internal
> feature-alignment representation for inference only; all catalog metadata anchors
> back to $I_{\text{raw}}$. The measured benefit is +0.052 mAP end-to-end with
> sharpness increased on 83.8% of scans."
